Wireless LC-Based Capacitive MEMS Pressure Microsensor

An end-to-end physics-to-machine-learning simulation of a passive, inductively-interrogated capacitive pressure sensor — from membrane mechanics to surrogate-driven inverse design.
Thin-plate mechanicsVariable-gap capacitance Modified-Wheeler inductanceInductive readout MLP surrogateInverse design

A pressure-sensitive silicon diaphragm changes a capacitance; that capacitance sits in an LC tank whose resonant frequency is read out wirelessly by an external coil — no battery on the sensor. This study models the full chain, then wraps a fast neural surrogate around the simulator to design geometries that hit a target performance. The signal path: pressure → deflection → capacitance → LC resonance → readout dip.

1Membrane Deflection

Clamped circular plate · small-deflection theory

A clamped silicon diaphragm bends under uniform pressure. Flexural rigidity and center deflection follow Kirchhoff thin-plate theory; the profile is flat at the rim and bulges at the center.

D = E·h³ / [12(1−ν²)]    w₀ = P·a⁴ / (64D)    w(r) = w₀(1 − r²/a²)²

At 50 kPa the center deflection (3.18 µm) exceeds h/5, so linear theory begins to overestimate — flagged, since real membranes stiffen as they stretch.

2Capacitance & LC Resonance

Parts 2–3 · variable-gap integration · spiral inductor

The deflected membrane is the top plate of a capacitor. Shrinking the gap raises capacitance, integrated numerically over the deformed profile. Pairing it with a square spiral inductor forms an LC tank whose resonance drops as pressure rises.

C(P) = ε₀∫₀ᵃ 2πr / [g₀ − w(r)] dr     f₀ = 1 / (2π√(LC))

C₀ = 2.32 pF, matching the flat-plate value exactly. Sensitivity ≈ 59 fF/kPa.

L = 899 nH. Above ~47 kPa the plates touch and those points are dropped, not crashed.

4Wireless Readout

Inductively-coupled reader · impedance dip tracks pressure

An external reader coil couples to the sensor tank. Sweeping the reader frequency, the magnitude of its input impedance shows a sharp dip at the sensor's resonance — so pressure is recovered purely through the reader.

Z_in(ω) = jωL_r + (ω²M²) / [R_s + jωL_s + 1/(jωC(P))], M = k√(L_rL_s)

Deeper coupling (higher k) yields a deeper dip; a design sweep over radius, thickness and gap flags geometries where plates touch or inductance leaves range.

5Surrogate Model & Inverse Design

Parts 5–6 · MLP surrogate · ~600× faster · verified inversion

Running the simulator across a Latin-Hypercube sample of geometries builds a dataset (171 valid designs). A small neural network learns geometry → performance, then is inverted to find geometries hitting a target sensitivity — every hit re-checked on the true simulator.

target −300 → verified −315 (5.1%)
target −450 → verified −461 (2.5%)
target −600 → verified −609 (1.5%)

Headline: the surrogate evaluates in ~2 µs vs ~1.3 ms for the full simulator — roughly 600× faster — making gradient-based inversion and large sweeps practical.

Caveats: small-deflection theory only; ideal parallel-plate capacitance (no fringing); lumped Wheeler inductance; surrogate trusted only inside its training range.
